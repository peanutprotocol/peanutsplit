Before

For comparison, and because two of these faults would otherwise repeat.

  • Knerd was painted solid black. The brand book and Split's own Title.tsx specify the three-layer offset. The new card sidesteps the question — the hero face is Roboto Flex 950, matching the landing page, and Knerd isn't used at all.
  • The body line had fallen back to a system font. Nobody caught it because silent fallbacks look deliberate.
  • Two thirds of the frame was empty, and nothing on it said what the product does.
  • The ground was yellow. Yellow is Split's primary-1, so it looked right in the config — but the hero band is pink and the yellow is reserved for the link card and CTA.

Shipped, and what the audit caught

Live on peanutsplit.com. Verified by fetching the page as Twitterbot and comparing bytes: the og:image tag, the file behind it, and the JSON-LD image are all the same card.

The first attempt changed the wrong file. og-default.png only feeds JSON-LD — seo.ts says so in its own comment. Scrapers read og:image, which came from a generated route, so Google would have seen the new card while Slack and Twitter kept the old one. The landing card is now a static opengraph-image.png inside the route segment, and a parity test fails if the two copies ever diverge.

Two claims on the card were wrong and are fixed. “clears it in the fewest transfers” trips the minimal-transfers CI rule and is untrue — the exact solver is bounded at 18 balances and goes greedy above that. “Works offline” is banned as a category claim and sat beside a Settle up CTA, which is the one action that needs a connection. “162 currencies” became “162 room currencies”, since only 156 convert.

Still open: the pwa.directory submission went out at 15:34 carrying “works offline”, before the audit ran. It sits in their review queue with that wording.
