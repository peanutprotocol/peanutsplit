# Split a bill when nobody wants to sign up

"Another app? Another signup?" they say. There is neither. A room is a web link, and whoever opens it types a name and starts adding what they paid.

The arithmetic was never the problem. You fronted the villa deposit and then the airport taxi, and dividing that by five is not going to get anybody a PhD. What is left is the message that starts with "sorry to be annoying", written five times, once each. No more!

Most people put it off for a week. Some never send it at all, and live with a quiet resentment that kills the friendship. Hand the asking to Peanut Split. The link goes in the group chat and the numbers sit on a page nobody has to argue with.

<Steps title="What everyone else has to do">
<Step title="Open the link">It opens in the browser already on the phone. There is no app store page in the way.</Step>
<Step title="Type a name">That is the whole of joining. Nothing to verify, nothing to remember.</Step>
<Step title="Add what they paid">The supermarket run, the boat tickets, the last round of drinks. Balances move as people type. <Cast name="ana" size="sm" caption="Ana typed her name in from the back of the taxi and added the fare before the bags were out of the boot." /></Step>
</Steps>

At the end the room nets the debts down, so the group makes two or three transfers instead of twenty. Two people settle however they settle, and one of them taps to record it. Just send the link. Everyone else does the typing.

## What Split is not good at

Split is free forever, with nothing to upgrade to. Peanut makes it to introduce people to Peanut, which is how Split gets paid for, so there is no paid tier here and nothing to move behind one later.

What you give up for having no accounts is everything an account does. There is no login, no password recovery, and no history that follows you to a new phone. The link is the key, so if the group loses it, the room is gone. Split does not check with a bank and cannot, so a settled row means somebody tapped to say they paid. It is built for a group rather than a crowd, up to twenty people.
