# Split a bill when nobody wants to sign up

Send one link. Nobody downloads anything and nobody makes an account, and the room adds up who paid what and who owes who.

The arithmetic was never the hard part. You fronted the villa deposit and then the airport taxi, and the job left over is the message that starts with "sorry to be annoying", written five times, once each. The link does that job instead. It goes in the group chat, the numbers sit on a page nobody has to argue with, and the ask has already happened without you making it.

<Steps title="What everyone else has to do">
<Step title="Open the link">It opens in whatever browser is already on the phone. There is no app store page in the way and nothing to install.</Step>
<Step title="Type a name">That is the whole of joining. No email, no password, no download, nothing to verify.</Step>
<Step title="Add what they paid">The taxi, the supermarket run, the second round. It all lands in the same room, and the balances move as people type. <Cast name="ana" size="sm" caption="Ana adds the taxi from the back seat, before anyone is out of the car." /></Step>
</Steps>

At the end the room nets the debts down, so the group makes two or three transfers instead of twenty. Two people settle however they settle, in cash or through a payment link, and one of them taps to record it. You stop being the group's accounts department.

## What Split is not good at

Split is free forever, with nothing to upgrade to. Peanut makes it to introduce people to Peanut, which is how Split gets paid for, so there is no paid tier here and nothing to move behind one later.

What you give up for having no accounts is everything an account does. There is no login, no password recovery, and no history that follows you to a new phone. The link is the key, so if the group loses it, the room is gone — keep it in the group chat rather than only in your own browser. Split does not check with a bank and cannot: a settled row means somebody tapped to say they paid. And it is built for a group rather than a crowd, up to twenty people.
