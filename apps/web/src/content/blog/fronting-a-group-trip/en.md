Somebody's card is holding this trip together and it is yours. The villa took a deposit in March and the rental car put a hold on the same card in June, months before anybody else has spent anything.

None of that is hard to work out. What is hard is the message afterwards, and the longer you leave it the more the number feels like a favour you are calling in rather than a bill you are passing on.

In a Starling Bank survey of 2,000 UK adults, 46% of the people who were owed money said they felt too awkward and embarrassed to ask for it, and the average wait to get it back ran to more than two months. Being owed money by friends is not an arithmetic problem.

So the work is not in the message. It is in arranging the trip so that by the time everyone is home, the number is old news and paying it is somebody else's next move.

## Before you pay anything, the room goes in the group chat

Open the room, then book. The deposit becomes the first line in a ledger everybody can see, dated the day it left your account, instead of something you have to explain in August.

It takes about ten seconds, there is no app store and no account, so there is no reason to wait until the trip feels real. Name the room after the trip and it has a picture on it before the first expense goes in.

<Steps title="Three things to settle before the first booking">
<Step title="Start the room, then pay the deposit">Paste the link in the group chat while everyone is still excited about going. A room that starts on the second day of the trip has already missed the flights, the villa and the deposit, which are the three biggest numbers in it.</Step>
<Step title="Hand the bookings out">You do not have to be the only card. Give the car to one person and the ferry to another. Three people fronting a third each is a trip. One person fronting all of it is a loan.</Step>
<Step title="Say what happens if somebody drops out">Deposits are rarely refundable, and this is the argument that actually ruins trips. Agree it now, while it is hypothetical and nobody has a name.</Step>
</Steps>

Put it in the room the day you pay it, at the amount you paid, and let it sit there for the four months before the trip. Everyone sees it every time they open the room, which does more for your chances of getting it back than any message you could write.
</Callout>

## The drip that nobody logs

The big bookings are the easy part. You remember a villa. What quietly empties your account is four days of "I'll get this one".

The airport coffees. The €22 taxi because you were nearest the door. The pastries on the way back from the beach. The round you got in because somebody had to. Each one is small enough that stopping to record it feels petty, and by Thursday the person who kept saying it is a couple of hundred down without noticing.

You said you would book the villa. Nobody asked you to run a small unsecured lending desk for four friends out of a current account.

So record them at the table, while the receipt is still in your hand. It takes longer to describe than to do. No signal on the beach is fine: an expense typed with no connection waits on your phone and goes when the connection comes back.

Enter what you actually paid, in the currency you paid it in. Split covers twelve currencies and converts at the day's rate, and that rate is fixed onto the expense when you add it, so the history does not move under you later. It is an indicative rate rather than your bank's.

## At the end, do not make the ask

By the time everyone is home the total is not news. Nobody has to be told what the trip cost, because they have watched it add up for a fortnight.

What is left is a pile of debts pointing in every direction, and paying those off one at a time is how a finished trip stays unfinished for a month. The room nets them against each other instead and gives you a short payment plan: two or three transfers instead of twenty, each one a single person paying a single person.

There is also a card at the end of it, with what the week cost, how long it ran and who fronted the most money. If you paid the villa deposit in March, [that last line](/blog/end-of-trip-expense-recap) is the only acknowledgement the trip is ever going to produce.

<Checklist title="What being paid back looks like when the room was open all along">
<ChecklistItem title="The number is not an announcement">Everybody watched it accumulate, so no part of the total needs defending.</ChecklistItem>
<ChecklistItem title="A short payment plan, not twenty errands">The debts net against each other, so the trip closes in two or three transfers and each one is one person's decision rather than a group negotiation.</ChecklistItem>
<ChecklistItem title="Nobody gets named">The room shows balances, not blame. There is never a reason to post a list of who has not paid yet, and doing it is how a finished trip becomes a grudge.</ChecklistItem>
<ChecklistItem title="You point instead of asking">The link is the message. That is the difference between "sorry to be annoying, could you send me €140" and "the room is up to date".</ChecklistItem>
</Checklist>

Recording a payment is one tap on the person who paid you. Settle however the two of you settle, with cash at the airport, a bank transfer or a Peanut link, and one of you taps to say it happened. Split records the payment, it does not make it.

## The honest bit

A few things Split will not do for you, and it is better to know them before the trip than during it.

- **It does not move the money.** A settled row means somebody tapped to say they paid, not that a payment cleared. Split does not check with a bank and cannot, which is fine among friends and worth knowing about your own ledger.
- **It will not chase anyone.** No reminder emails, no nudges into the group chat. If somebody goes quiet, that part is still yours.
- **The link is the key.** There is no login and no password recovery, so if the group loses the link, the room is gone. Keep it in the group chat rather than only in your own browser.
- **It holds up to twenty people.** A group, not a conference.
- **It is free forever, with nothing to upgrade to.** Peanut makes it to introduce people to Peanut, which is how Split gets paid for, so there is no paid tier waiting to be sold to you halfway through a trip.
