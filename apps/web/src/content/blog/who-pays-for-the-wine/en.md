# Who pays for the wine at dinner?

If some people at dinner did not drink, one option is to divide the wine among those who did and split the food separately. Agree the approach before ordering so everyone knows what they are sharing.

An equal split can also work when people ordered similar amounts or are happy to cover a difference. The choice depends on what your group agrees to pay for.

## Three ways to divide the bill

**Split everything equally.** Divide the total by the number of people. This takes little work, but someone who ordered less pays part of the others' meal. Check that everyone is comfortable with that, particularly when the difference is large or the same person regularly pays more than they consumed.

**Separate the drinks.** Divide the food among everyone who shared it and the alcohol among the people who drank it. This handles a common difference without assigning every dish. It still requires agreement about shared bottles and people who only had one glass.

**Assign each item.** Each person pays for what they ordered, with shared dishes divided among those who ate them. This is useful when orders vary a lot. It takes more checking, and you still need a way to divide service charges or other shared costs.

Someone can also offer to cover another person's share. Make that offer explicit so it is clear whether repayment is expected.

## A worked example

Six people share €120 of food. Four also share €60 of wine. The bill is €180.

- Food: €120 ÷ 6 = €20 each.
- Wine: €60 ÷ 4 = €15 per drinker.
- Each drinker pays €35; each non-drinker pays €20.

The six payments add up to €180. An equal split would be €30 each, so separating the wine changes each person's share by a noticeable amount.

For a small difference, itemising may take more effort than your group wants to spend. You can separate the wine and leave shared starters together, or agree an equal split. There is no need to track every bite to choose a useful rule.

## Agree before ordering

You can say: ‘Shall we split the food equally and put the wine on the people drinking it?’ If you order a bottle for the table, check who wants to share it before including them in the cost.

If you arrive after others have eaten, say that you will pay for your own order. If the bill has already arrived without an agreement, suggest the division and check it with the table before asking anyone to pay.

The same principle applies to other shared costs, such as [a holiday house with different room sizes](/guides/split-holiday-house-per-person-or-per-room): agree what each person is paying for while they can still decide whether to take part.

## Record who paid

Peanut Split can record the bill and each person's share, then offset that against what they already paid. A room is shared through a link. The group still chooses the division and sends the money separately.

After a repayment, record it in the room. Split does not verify it with a bank. If someone has not paid by an agreed date, use a [direct repayment message](/guides/ask-a-friend-to-pay-you-back) with the amount and payment details.
