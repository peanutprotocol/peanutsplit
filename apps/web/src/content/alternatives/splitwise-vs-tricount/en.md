## The short answer

Pick **Tricount** if your group is in Europe, adds a lot of expenses, and spends in more than one currency. Nothing caps its free tier and it converts currencies without a subscription.

Pick **Splitwise** if you want a web app as well as a phone app, if everything is in one currency, and if expenses arrive a few at a time rather than a whole trip's worth in one sitting.

The rest of the page is what each one's own site says, quoted, so you can check the working. The table has a third column and it is ours, which normally settles the outcome in advance. It loses the offline row.

## What each one says it is for

<Quote source="splitwise.com">
Keep track of your shared expenses and balances with housemates, trips, groups, friends, and family.
</Quote>

<Quote source="tricount.com">
tricount helps you keep track of who paid what. Invite your friends, add your expenses, focus on the fun.
</Quote>

Same job, described the same way. The tracking is not the decision.

## 1. The free tier, and the number nobody agrees on

Splitwise caps how many expenses a free account can add in a day, and says so itself:

<Quote source="feedback.splitwise.com">
Splitwise has introduced a daily expense limit for our free tier.
</Quote>

That page does not give the number. The help centre does, inside the description of the Pro feature that removes it:

<Quote source="kb.splitwise.com">
Add as many expenses as you need without hitting a limit (free users can add up to 4 expenses each day).
</Quote>

<Callout title="Tricount's comparison page and Splitwise's help centre disagree">
Tricount's page puts the Splitwise cap at "5 per day". The figure in the quote above is Splitwise's own, on Splitwise's own site, and it is the one to rely on. Tricount's table carries its own warning: "Comparison based on publicly available information as of 01/04/2025. Please check the respective websites for the latest details."
</Callout>

Splitwise's own advice, if you hit the cap mid-trip, is to wait or hand the job to somebody else:

<Quote source="feedback.splitwise.com">
Alternatively, you can add more expenses as soon as the daily limit resets, or ask a friend to add the expenses on your behalf.
</Quote>

Tricount has no equivalent cap and says so on its comparison page, where it marks its own free tier "No ads, no limits" and Splitwise's "With ads and limits". Its homepage repeats the claim:

<Quote source="tricount.com">
Experience the easiest way to track group expenses, 100% free!
</Quote>

Splitwise lists "A totally ad-free experience" among its Pro features, not its core ones.

**Who this decides it for:** anyone who enters a week of expenses in one evening. On the last night of a trip that cap is a wall.

## 2. Currencies

This decides more groups than the cap does, and most comparisons skip it.

Splitwise's homepage lists "100+ currencies" under core features and "Currency conversion" under Pro. Those are two different things. Its help centre spells out which one you get:

<Quote source="kb.splitwise.com">
Only Pro subscribers can convert, but all group members will see expenses in their converted form.
</Quote>

The feature that tidies the balances does not cross currencies either:

<Quote source="kb.splitwise.com">
If your group uses more than one currency, Simplify Debts works within each currency separately. It does not combine or convert balances across currencies.
</Quote>

A free Splitwise group that spent in euros and baht settles two sets of debts, not one.

Tricount converts on the free tier:

<Quote source="tricount.com">
tricount automatically converts expenses into a single currency for easy tracking.
</Quote>

**Who this decides it for:** anyone whose trip crosses a border. On the free tier, this row is Tricount's.

## 3. Accounts, apps and who has to join

Splitwise wants everyone in the group to have a login:

<Quote source="kb.splitwise.com">
We think it's important that everyone in a Splitwise group can log in and see their balance.
</Quote>

There is one way round it. You can add someone by name only, as a placeholder friend, from the web app. An unclaimed invite does not get chased forever:

<Quote source="kb.splitwise.com">
We'll send one invite, one reminder, and then leave them alone.
</Quote>

Tricount marks itself "No Registration" on its own comparison page, and shares by link:

<Quote source="tricount.com">
Share your tricount link with your friends. Everyone can add their own expenses, see what's already been added.
</Quote>

The other half of this row is where each one runs. Splitwise's homepage says "Free for iPhone, Android, and web." Tricount's buttons say "Download for free" and "Download tricount now".

**Who this decides it for:** anyone whose group contains one person who will not install anything. Neither solves that cleanly. Tricount wants the download, Splitwise wants the login.

## 4. Settling up

Both stop in the same place. They give you the number and hand you to a payment rail.

<Quote source="splitwise.com">
Settle up with a friend and record any cash or online payment.
</Quote>

<Quote source="tricount.com">
Send a payment request straight from the app and get paid directly to your bank account.
</Quote>

Splitwise records whatever you did. Tricount names the rail, a bank account, and sells a card next to it:

<Quote source="tricount.com">
Pay with your bunq card and the expense gets automatically added to your tricount.
</Quote>

Tricount is owned by bunq, a bank, and the card-based automatic tracking runs in some countries only. Its own comparison page qualifies that row "in NL, FR, DE, ES and IT".

**Who this decides it for:** nobody, mostly. Neither one moves the money unless your group already shares a rail.

## The case where neither answer is right

Both of the above assume two things about your group. Read them as a test.

<Checklist title="If both of these are true, either app is fine">
<ChecklistItem title="Everyone will make an account or install an app">One asks for a login, the other for a download. Somebody does one of those before anybody can add an expense.</ChecklistItem>
<ChecklistItem title="You share a way to pay each other">A transfer that reaches everyone, or cash in the same room. Tricount's settle-up names a bank account. Splitwise records whatever you managed on your own.</ChecklistItem>
</Checklist>

If one of them is false, you are not choosing between Splitwise and Tricount. You are choosing between a spreadsheet and giving up.

## What Split does instead

That group still owes each other money, and the money does not stop mattering because neither app fits. What happens instead is that nobody keeps a list, everybody remembers a different total, and the person who paid the most decides it is not worth the argument. Send that group a Split room instead.

A room is a web link. It asks for neither the login nor the download: you send it, and the people who open it are in. The link is the key, so it belongs in the group chat rather than in one person's browser.

A room counts in one currency and takes expenses in any of twelve, converted at the day's rate. The balances net down to a short payment plan.

Settling up is where Split is smallest. Two people settle however they settle, and one of them taps to record it. Split does not check with a bank and cannot. It takes your word for it, the way a shared note does.

## When Splitwise or Tricount is the better tool

Take Splitwise if somebody wants receipt scanning, charts of what the group spends, or search back through old bills, and take it if your group is already there and never comes near the cap.

Take Tricount if you are in one of the countries where the card files the expense as you pay it, and if everybody banks somewhere a payment request can reach.

Both are bigger products with more in them, and a group that would have been happy with either should use either. Split has no card and no bank behind it.

## Good to know

Split is free forever, with nothing to upgrade to. Peanut makes it to introduce people to Peanut, which is how Split gets paid for, so there is no second version to sell you and no plan to build one.
