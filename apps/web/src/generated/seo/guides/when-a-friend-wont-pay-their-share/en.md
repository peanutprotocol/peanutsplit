Before making a court claim over an unpaid shared bill, check what was agreed, what remains unpaid
and what evidence you have. Compare the amount with the court fees and the chance of recovering it.
This page covers England and Wales and provides general information, not advice on an individual
case. Other UK jurisdictions use different procedures.

## When a friend will not pay their share

Your agreement to split a cost and your responsibility to the supplier or landlord are separate
questions. For example, joint tenants can be responsible for the whole rent even if they agreed
different shares between themselves. [Shelter explains joint tenancy liability](https://england.shelter.org.uk/housing_advice/private_renting/joint_tenancies/).

For an energy bill or another account, check who is responsible under that agreement. Do not assume
a private split changes what the supplier can ask you to pay.

Suppose four people agreed to share a £180 monthly bill equally. Each share is £45. If you cover one
person's share for five months, the unpaid amount is £45 × 5 = £225. That calculation explains the
amount; evidence of the agreement is a separate part of any claim.

## Taking a friend to small claims court

In England and Wales, a money claim goes through the county court. Before starting, check the
[Citizens Advice guidance on small claims](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/law-and-courts/legal-system/small-claims/deciding-whether-to-make-a-small-claim/).
It covers evidence and whether the person can pay. A judgment will be of limited use if there is
no money to recover.

Court issue fees depend on the amount claimed, including any interest. The following bands were
checked on 9 September 2026:

| Claim amount | Issue fee |
|---|---|
| Up to £300 | £35 |
| £300.01–£500 | £50 |
| £500.01–£1,000 | £70 |
| £1,000.01–£1,500 | £80 |
| £1,500.01–£3,000 | £115 |
| £3,000.01–£5,000 | £205 |
| £5,000.01–£10,000 | £455 |

The £225 example would cost £35 to issue. Further fees can apply for a hearing or enforcement.
People on low incomes or certain benefits may qualify for help with fees.
[Source: GOV.UK court fees](https://www.gov.uk/make-court-claim-for-money/court-fees).

<Callout type="info">
**Mediation may be required.** GOV.UK says that if a claim of £10,000 or less is disputed, you will
be told to attend court-arranged mediation. Failing to attend when required can lead to sanctions.
Check the instructions for your case in the [official mediation guide](https://www.gov.uk/make-court-claim-for-money/mediation).
</Callout>

## Winning a claim does not guarantee payment

If the person does not pay after a court order, enforcement requires a further application and fee.
The available method depends on their circumstances. Read the
[GOV.UK enforcement guide](https://www.gov.uk/make-court-claim-for-money/enforce-a-judgment)
and get advice before choosing a route.

## What records to keep

Keep records from the first shared bill:

- The agreement about who would pay which share.
- Bills and receipts showing the expense, amount and date.
- Records of what each person paid and what remains unpaid.
- Messages about disputed amounts or agreed payment dates.

Before a dispute reaches court, try a clear request for the outstanding amount. These
[repayment messages](/guides/ask-a-friend-to-pay-you-back) include a reminder you can adapt.
Citizens Advice can help you assess whether to take a claim further.
