Split a holiday cottage by room if bedroom size and privacy matter most. Split by person if the
group wants the cost to reflect how many people are staying. You can adjust either method for
children or shorter stays, but agree those adjustments before anyone pays.

## Holiday cottage: pay per person or per room

This example uses a £2,080 cottage for seven nights. Six adults and three children, aged 14, 10 and
6, stay in four bedrooms. The households are a family of four, a family of three and a couple.

### Split by room

Give each bedroom a weight. Here, a standard double is one share, the ensuite is 1.25 and the bunk
room is 0.75. These are example weights for the group to agree.

The weights total four, so each share costs £2,080 ÷ 4 = £520.

| Bedroom | Weight | Cost | Occupants |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ensuite double | 1.25 | £650 | Two adults from the family of four |
| Double | 1 | £520 | Two adults from the family of three |
| Double | 1 | £520 | The couple |
| Bunk room | 0.75 | £390 | Three children |
| Total | 4 | £2,080 | |

Split the bunk room by its occupants: £260 for the family with two children and £130 for the family
with one. The household totals are £910, £650 and £520.

### Split by person

In this version, adults and children aged 12 or over count as one share. Under-12s count as half.
The family of four has the two younger children; the family of three has the 14-year-old.

There are eight shares, so each costs £2,080 ÷ 8 = £260.

| Household | Shares | Calculation | Pays |
|---|---|---|---|
| Family of four | 3 | Two adults and two half shares | £780 |
| Family of three | 3 | Two adults and one full child share | £780 |
| Couple | 2 | Two adults | £520 |
| Total | 8 | | £2,080 |

<Callout type="info">
**The total is the same.** Both methods collect £2,080. The room split charges the family of four
£130 more and the family of three £130 less than the person split. The couple pays £520 in both.
</Callout>

## How to count children in a holiday split

A half share is a choice, not a rule about what children cost. Agree the age cutoff and weight with
the group. Reducing one household's share increases what the others pay when the booking total is
fixed.

You can use different rules for different expenses. A child might count as half a share for the
house, a full share for groceries and no share for wine.

## When a household stays fewer nights

Suppose the couple stays five nights and both families stay seven. Using the person weights above,
multiply each household's shares by its nights: 21 + 21 + 10 = 52 share-nights.

Each share-night costs £2,080 ÷ 52 = £40.

| Household | Shares | Nights | Share-nights | Pays |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Family of four | 3 | 7 | 21 | £840 |
| Family of three | 3 | 7 | 21 | £840 |
| Couple | 2 | 5 | 10 | £400 |
| Total | | | 52 | £2,080 |

The couple pays £120 less than in the full-week person split. Each family pays £60 more. Agree that
change explicitly: the booking still costs £2,080. If someone cancels entirely, compare the
[options for a group-trip cancellation](/guides/someone-drops-out-of-a-group-trip).

## Divide groceries and alcohol separately

For this example, the group agrees equal food shares and charges wine only to the people drinking
it. A different group may choose child weights for food too.

| Purchase | Total | Participants | Each |
|---|---|---|---|
| Groceries | £405 | 9 | £45 |
| Wine | £96 | 4 | £24 |

## Keep the agreed method with the expenses

things, Peanut Split can keep the expenses in one room and suggest a short payment plan.

while preserving each person's net balance.
