The room where the trip's expenses live says you owe Jules £30, and Jules is the one person there you
have never paid for anything with; pay a number you cannot account for and you stop trusting every
other number in the list. Some people say nothing about it at all, quietly stop adding their
receipts, and let the trip finish that way. Let Split by Peanut show the working. It nets the debts
down before it asks anybody to send anything.

Nothing is broken. The debts the group already had were set against each other, and the name on your
payment moved when they were.

## Why the app says you owe someone you never paid

Three of you shared a flat for a week, and two expenses went into the room.

- Bea paid £60 for the taxi from the airport, shared with you. Jules landed the next morning and was
  not in it.
- Jules paid £80 for the cleaning at the end of the stay, shared with Bea. You had already left for
  your flight.

Pair by pair, that is two debts. You owe Bea £30, half the taxi. Bea owes Jules £40, half the
cleaning. Jules owes you nothing, and you owe Jules nothing.

Now line the two debts up against each other and the middle drops out. Bea is owed £30 and owes £40,
so Bea is £10 down. You are £30 down. Jules is £40 up. Two payments produce exactly that: you send
Jules £30, and Bea sends Jules £10.

You owe Jules because that is where the arithmetic came out. You are £30 down either way, which is
what you were down when the taxi door shut.

## What "simplify debts" means

Simplify debts is the name this step goes by. Your debt to Bea and Bea's debt to Jules cancel as far
as they can, and what is left over is a payment from you to Jules. Splitwise defines its own version
this way.

> It never changes anyone's total balance — it only optimizes the payment paths.

*Splitwise help centre, kb.splitwise.com/balances-and-expenses/what-is-simplify-debts, checked
August 2026*

The wording is theirs. The arithmetic is the one above: what you are down did not move, and the name
attached to it did.

### Check the room before you pay

- Open the expense list rather than the balance on its own.
- Find the lines you were in and check your share against the receipt.
- Ask the payer when a line looks wrong, before anybody sends anything.
- Keep the room open until the group is all square.

## Pay the person the plan names

The other route is the chain: you pay Bea, Bea waits for it to arrive, and Bea then passes it on to
Jules. Every leg needs somebody to remember it and somebody to chase it, which means somebody
writing [the message that asks for it back](/guides/ask-a-friend-to-pay-you-back). Spare yourself
that!

Settle it however you like: cash, a bank transfer, Peanut. Then record the payment in the room.
Split records the payment, it does not make it. It does not verify with any bank and cannot.

## When paying each other directly is the better tool

Two people and one dinner need no netting at all, and the plan will hand back the debt you already
knew about. Paying directly also wins when the money has to be seen to reach the person who fronted
it. Splitwise makes its own version optional: "You can toggle this feature on or off from your
group's settings." (Splitwise help centre,
kb.splitwise.com/balances-and-expenses/what-is-simplify-debts, checked August 2026.) Where the two
apps stop on this and on everything else is
[Splitwise vs Settle Up](/guides/splitwise-vs-settle-up).

What the room hands you is a plan rather than a proof: it does not show the route it took to get
there, and it does not present the list as the shortest one that could exist.
