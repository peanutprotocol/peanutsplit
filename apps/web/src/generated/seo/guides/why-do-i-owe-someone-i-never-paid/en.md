A bill-splitting app may suggest paying someone who did not cover your expense because it combines
the group's debts. This changes who receives the payment while keeping each person's net balance
the same. Check the underlying expenses before paying an amount you do not recognise.

## Why you owe someone who never paid for you

Suppose Bea paid £60 for a taxi shared with you, and Jules paid £80 for cleaning shared with Bea.
You owe Bea £30 for the taxi. Bea owes Jules £40 for cleaning.

Those debts can be settled in either of these ways:

| Payment | Direct debts | Simplified plan |
|---|---|---|
| You pay Bea | £30 | £0 |
| You pay Jules | £0 | £30 |
| Bea pays Jules | £40 | £10 |

In both plans, you pay £30 in total. Bea pays £10 net, and Jules receives £40. Paying Jules directly
removes the need for Bea to receive your £30 and pass it on.

## What “simplify debts” means

Debt simplification combines what each person owes and is owed. Splitwise
[describes its feature](https://kb.splitwise.com/balances-and-expenses/what-is-simplify-debts)
as changing payment routes without changing total balances.

Peanut Split uses the group's balances to suggest a short payment plan. The suggested payee depends
on expenses across the group, including expenses you did not share.

Before paying, check your participation and share in each expense. A correct calculation can still
use an incorrectly entered expense, so ask the payer about anything that looks wrong.

## Paying and recording the payment

Agree which plan the group will follow before sending money. If payment is overdue, you can adapt
these [repayment messages](/guides/ask-a-friend-to-pay-you-back).

Pay by your usual method, then record it in Split. Split does not send money or verify bank
payments. A paid status means someone recorded the payment.

## When you prefer to pay the original payer

For two people sharing one bill, no debt simplification is needed. A larger group may also prefer
direct payments so each person receives money from the people they paid for.

Splitwise's setting is optional, but its help centre advises leaving it on after payments have
already been made. Switching can change how those payments match the original debts.
[Source: Splitwise help centre](https://kb.splitwise.com/balances-and-expenses/what-is-simplify-debts),
checked 9 September 2026. Do not assume every app has the same setting; see the
[Splitwise and Settle Up comparison](/guides/splitwise-vs-settle-up).
